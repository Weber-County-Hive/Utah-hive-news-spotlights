With Pineview Drawn Down, the Wells That Once Watered Ogden Are Showing Again

Ogden's pipeline project has lowered Pineview Reservoir to some of its lowest levels in decades, uncovering pipes, wood and an old intake from the artesian wells that supplied the city's water more than a century ago.

Published Oct. 18, 2026 · Updated Oct. 18, 2026 · Ran in the Sunday Buzz, Oct. 18, 2026
From local news reports

Long before Pineview Reservoir filled the Ogden Valley, the land near where the North and Middle Forks of the Ogden River met was known as Artesian Park. Starting around 1914, Ogden City drew much of its water from flowing artesian wells there, according to the Ogden Valley Watershed Action Group, writing in The Ogden Valley News. The water ran into a pipeline that carried it down Ogden Canyon to the city.

Pineview Reservoir was built in the 1930s. Some wells were plugged and others were extended to higher ground before the water rose, and Ogden later moved to a newer well field next to the reservoir. A 2019 Utah Geological Survey study found records of at least 51 wells drilled in the Artesian Park area.

This year Ogden City is replacing the pipelines that carry its water from the valley wells down the canyon: a 55-year-old line under the reservoir and a 100-year-old pipe through Ogden Canyon. The Salt Lake Tribune reported in June that the old line leaked about 1 million gallons of drinking water a day and serves about 120,000 people. To do the work, Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, which operates Pineview Dam, planned to lower the reservoir about 50 feet, to its lowest level since the 1970s.

As the water dropped, the old Artesian Park came back into view. In September, the watershed group shared a photo of what appears to be one of the historic wells, still flowing. In October, KSL reported on remnants of buildings, pipes and an intake structure, where water from the wells came together before heading down the canyon.

“It’s nice to see that somebody from 1914 preserved something so well that we can still access it today.”Tonya Gonzales, Ogden deputy city recorder, to KSL (Oct. 13, 2026)

Gonzales, who works with the city’s old records, said she recognized the intake from them. “It was actually where all the wells pumped the water to that intake, and then that intake fed the water line that came down the canyon,” she told KSL.

The pipeline work must be finished by Jan. 15, according to Weber Basin. When the reservoir refills depends on next winter’s snow, Ogden City told the Tribune. Until then, the watershed group has encouraged people to photograph and document what’s showing, which could help groundwater scientists and water managers later.
